@yetone dorokuma reported in #5477: a routing group moved a conversation from provider A to provider B mid-session, and the next turn came back HTTP 400 from B — an invalid-request-class error over a resource that isn't B's — while the same conversation kept working on A. They traced the outbound body carrying a `conversation` / `previous_response_id` pointer to the previous turn's stored response, made under A's account, and asked that magpie check who the history belongs to before the pointer rides along.

**Root cause**: on a translated route magpie never writes such a pointer — `buildResponses` (`internal/gateway/responses.go`) assembles the input from the parsed conversation and sets `store: false` — and an id magpie doesn't hold is refused there with `previous_response_not_found` before anything is sent (`gateway.go` attempt's translate path). On a relay — a group whose members are served on Responses is relayed the request as it is (`#1594`: Pi, omp, droid, dsh) — the pointer in the body is the client's own: the id of the reply the previous member handed back. When magpie holds that turn (`store: true`) `previousResponse` gives the conversation whole and deletes the pointer, and magpie's own delta attachments are route-guarded (`prevDelta` fires only for `prevKeeps` providers on the same `prevRoute`). But a client that says `store: false` has nothing kept — and an id magpie doesn't hold is relayed as written, for the provider it names may hold the response: one member of a group does, the one that answered the turn. The member that didn't refused the request whole, and the agent was left with that 400. The relay had no recovery, where magpie's own delta turns are asked again whole on a `prevRefused` refusal.

**The fix**: the relay that refuses over the pointer it carried (OpenAI's own `previous_response_not_found` shape, `prevRefused` in `internal/gateway/gateway.go` passthrough) is asked again once without the pointer. The input a Responses client sends carries the conversation whole, so B answers with the history it needs — dorokuma's own observation that later turns back on A succeed shows the client holds the whole conversation too. A pointer-only client loses nothing that wasn't already lost: magpie holds no history to inline for it. A refusal over anything else is asked again by no one new — the 400 stays the vendor's — and the working relay to the provider that holds the response goes as written as before. Following the reporter's own analysis: magpie can't know who holds an id it doesn't (`store: false` keeps nothing to attribute it with), so the check they asked for can't be made before sending; the honest equivalent is this retry — as written first (the holder answers as before), then without the pointer on a refusal over it. Their option (b), keeping the conversation on the owning provider, is what a group's affinity already does for the member that answered (`affinity.json`); the retry covers the moves it doesn't.
