**So, to the question asked — "is there a setting to cap sessions or auto-close idle ones?" — yes, and it shipped before your build**: `magpie claude-code runs N` (or `claudeRunsMost` in settings.json) caps how many Claude Code processes magpie keeps for Claude subscriptions; a new ask that finds the room full first lets an idle run go (session shelved, next turn resumes it), then waits 2 minutes, then answers 503 and routing moves on (#2956, in v0.1.2899 since 2026-10-20). On your 4 GB Lightsail box, `magpie claude-code runs 6` bounds magpie's share at about six processes — at your measured ~250 MiB each, ~1.5 GiB worst case, whatever the auto cap believes; already-idle ones end as room is needed for a new ask, or within the hour as always. Your build also already lets go of any single run holding over its memory budget once its turn ends (a093e9c3, #3256, in v0.1.2899). The remaining growth you saw in `magpie sessions` is transcript files, not processes — the bridge ones are removed when nothing will resume them, and what a bridged request still leaves on disk is #3983, open and tracked.

**What I can't settle from here, and what would settle it**: whether the 11 `claude` processes were mostly magpie's or the downstream agent's own. If you still have the box in that state (or can walk it back there), two things decide it:

1. `magpie claude-code runs` output while memory is tight — it names magpie's processes only, with RSS and state.
2. whether the downstream Claude Code agent ran on that same 4 GB box.

If the first shows most of the eleven were magpie's kept runs, then the 128 MiB sizing is wrong by 2× on current Claude Code and the auto cap deserves re-sizing from the *kept* footprint rather than the start one — that would trade capacity on big rooms (512 MiB would hold 1, not 2) for not dying on small ones, and it's @yetone's measurement call, made with your numbers in hand. The transcripts piling in `magpie sessions` from bridged requests are #3983 either way.
