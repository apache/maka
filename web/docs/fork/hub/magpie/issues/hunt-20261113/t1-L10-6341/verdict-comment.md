Every piece of the behaviour you are asking for is already what the gateway does on a routing group. Mapping your three asks onto what ships today, then to what would tell us why your two requests didn't rotate:

**1. A short cool-down that honors the vendor's wait.** A 429 whose answer names a time rests that member until then (`vendorSaidWhen`, `internal/gateway/routing.go:2092`; the rest is set through `restAfterMarked`, `routing.go:1404`), and a member resting is answered from the rest without asking upstream again, the first back-time going back as Retry-After (`timedHeld`, `internal/gateway/fallback.go:1456`; `quotaHeld`, `fallback.go:1423` — built for #1772: four keys out for a week, each refused on every request). Kiro's rate wording rides its plugin for exactly this (0.1.30 "says an account's request rate plainly and stops the 1/2/4s re-asks", 0.1.32 "waits out a 429 throttle as the Kiro IDE does" — the commits your error text comes from). A 429 that names no time rests nobody — a relay's moment of saturation must not park a member (`internal/gateway/gateway.go:5311`). Your Kiro quote ("please wait before trying again", `USER_REQUEST_RATE_EXCEEDED`) carries no time, so on today's code Kiro would not be parked — and would not need to be, for the next point.

**2. Rotating to the next candidate within the same request.** This is the group loop's main path, and it has a test in exactly your shape: `TestRoutingGroups` (`internal/gateway/group_test.go:240`) puts two members in a user-made group, the first answers 429, and the assert is that the reply comes from the second member's account, in the same request, the first member tried once. A member's rest is cool-down bookkeeping, not the end of the request — the loop goes on to the next candidate either way.

**3. An error only when no candidate is left, with Retry-After.** That is the `last` branch of the same loop (`internal/gateway/gateway.go:5306`): only after the final candidate fails does the 429 go back, said as the vendor's words with "not asked again before <time>" and a Retry-After header naming when the first of the rested candidates is back. `TestOpenRouterFreeAccountRateLimitKeepsProviderRest` (`group_test.go:211`) walks the rest side of it.

**Why your two sessions went back with Kiro's 429 anyway.** The likeliest reading is in your own sentence — 「候选链里如果有其他可用账号/模型，没有发生轮换重试」 ("if there were other available accounts/models in the chain, no rotation happened"), said conditionally. `group/task-auto` is a group of your own making: magpie's found groups are spelled `auto/<model>` (kept internally as `group/auto-<slug>`, `internal/provider/autoref.go`), so `task-auto` with the name 「自动选择 · 任务与额度」 is a saved group, and its members and their order are on your Groups page. If Kiro is the only member, or every other member was itself resting or out of allowance at 20:54, the 429 you saw is the `last` branch — the designed answer, with nothing to rotate to. Two sessions dying 26 seconds apart fits that reading: each hit the same single-candidate chain.

**What would tell us which it was**: the routing trace of one of those requests — the route card in Settings › Usage lists every `try` with its candidate and error, so a rotation that didn't happen shows as a single try; the magpie version (your report carries none, and this machinery has been tightened repeatedly through November); and the task-auto member list.

**Where the real gaps still are** — your links name them well, and they are the live seams, not this ask: #6318 (every candidate answering a no-time 429 spins the request 30s–3m30s before answering — "rests nobody" prevents wrong parks, not the spin), #6188 (a weekly run-out re-asked on short rests instead of waiting its window), #6166 (a model's limit painting the provider). And the opposite trade — #4030, keeping a request on its own account through a transient rest for the cache's sake — is where a failover-versus-cache-cost knob would sit if one is ever wanted; today the dial is set per the scene: groups rotate, a pinned conversation waits out a transient rest on its own account.
